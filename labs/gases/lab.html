Bench card: the equations
Gas Bench · Pharmacy
UnitsP in atm, V in L, n in mol, T in K
ConstantsR = 0.0821 L·atm/(K·mol) · 1 atm = 760 mmHg · T (K) = t (°C) + 273
Based onChang, Chemistry (10th ed.), Chapter 5

1 · Cylinder: Boyle’s law

P₁V₁ = P₂V₂  (T and n constant)
  1. The gauge shows pressure above the air. True (absolute) pressure = gauge + 1 atm.
  2. Gas the cylinder holds, at 1 atm: V₂ = P₁V₁ ÷ 1 atm.
  3. When “empty”, the cylinder still holds its own volume of gas at 1 atm. Usable gas = total − cylinder volume.
  4. Minutes = usable litres ÷ flow (L/min).

2 · Hot can: pressure and temperature

P₁ ÷ T₁ = P₂ ÷ T₂  (V and n constant)
  1. Change every temperature to kelvin first: T = t + 273.
  2. P₂ = P₁ × T₂ ÷ T₁.

Label: Pressurised container: protect from sunlight and do not expose to temperatures exceeding 50 °C.

3 · Propellant: PV = nRT and molar mass

n = PV ÷ RT    ℳ = mass ÷ n
  1. Change mmHg to atm: ÷ 760. Change ml to L: ÷ 1000.
  2. Find n, then ℳ. Compare with the table.

4 · Peroxide: gas collected over water

2H₂O₂ → 2H₂O + O₂    P(O₂) = P(atm) − P(H₂O)
  1. Subtract the water vapour pressure at the water’s temperature.
  2. n(O₂) = PV ÷ RT, with P in atm and V in L. ×1000 for mmol.
  3. n(H₂O₂) = 2 × n(O₂).
  4. % w/v = grams of H₂O₂ in the sample ÷ ml of sample × 100. ℳ(H₂O₂) = 34.01 g/mol.

Ideal: P = nRT ÷ V    Real: P = nRT ÷ (V − nb) − an²/V²

Hints: four ideas
Gas laws work only in kelvin: add 273.
A gauge reads above the air. Add 1 atm for the true pressure.
Gas over water also holds water vapour. Subtract it.
Compare real with ideal to see which correction wins.
