Bench card: the method
Ring Bench · Pharmacy
Heat of hydrogenationcyclohexene: 120 kJ/mol (28.6 kcal/mol) for one C=C
Based onBruice, Organic Chemistry (4th ed.), Chapters 7, 15 and 16; PHCH 4123 Unit 5 notes

1 · Name it

  1. Special parent names: CH₃ toluene, OH phenol, NH₂ aniline, COOH benzoic acid, CHO benzaldehyde, CH=CH₂ styrene.
  2. Other single groups: group name + “benzene” (bromobenzene, nitrobenzene). One group needs no number.
  3. Alkyl chain: 6 carbons or fewer, benzene is the parent (ethylbenzene). 7 or more, the chain is the parent and the ring is phenyl (2-phenylheptane).
  4. Two or more groups: the parent group’s carbon is C1. Number round the ring for the lowest set of numbers. List groups A to Z; on a tie, the first in the alphabet gets the lower number.
  5. ortho (o) = 1,2 · meta (m) = 1,3 · para (p) = 1,4.

2 · Aromatic?

  1. Count π electrons in the ring: 2 for each C=C. A negative ring carbon adds 2 (a lone pair in a p orbital); a positive one adds 0 (an empty p orbital).
  2. Every ring atom needs a p orbital. An sp³ CH₂ in the ring breaks the π cloud.
  3. Hückel’s rule: 4n + 2 π electrons, that is 2, 6, 10 …
  4. Aromatic only if both 2 and 3 are true (and the ring is flat).

3 · Stability

expected heat = (number of C=C) × 120 kJ/mol
extra stability = expected − measured

A large extra stability (benzene: about 152 kJ/mol, 36 kcal/mol) means aromatic. A few kJ/mol comes from conjugation only.

4 · Bromine water

  1. Alkenes (and any C=C outside a ring) add Br₂: the orange colour disappears.
  2. Benzene does not react: the orange colour stays (it moves into the benzene layer).
  3. To brominate the ring: Br₂ with an FeBr₃ catalyst. This is substitution: one H is swapped for Br, and HBr forms.

5 · Predict the reaction

ReactionReagent + catalystElectrophileBy-product
HalogenationBr₂ + FeBr₃ (or Cl₂ + FeCl₃)Br⁺ (Cl⁺)HBr (HCl)
NitrationHNO₃ + H₂SO₄NO₂⁺H₂O
Sulfonationconcentrated H₂SO₄, heat⁺SO₃HH₂O
Friedel–Crafts acylationRCOCl + AlCl₃RCO⁺ (acylium ion)HCl
Friedel–Crafts alkylationRCl + AlCl₃R⁺HCl

Step 1 (slow): the ring’s π electrons attack the electrophile and a carbocation forms. Step 2 (fast): that same carbon loses H⁺ and the ring is aromatic again.
