1 · Purpose and scope

To confirm that the tablets contain chlorpheniramine maleate (identification) and to limit its impurities (related substances) by thin-layer chromatography.

2 · Specification

Identification (BP). Under UV 254 nm, the two principal spots of Solution (1) correspond to those of Solution (2). After spraying, the principal spot of Solution (1) corresponds to that of Solution (2).

Related substances (BP). Any secondary spot of Solution (1) is not more intense than the spot of Solution (2) (0.2%).

Trainer’s note: “corresponds” means the same Rf and the same colour. Spot size alone proves nothing.

3 · Plate, reagents and detection

PlateSilica gel GF254. Heat at 105 °C for 30 minutes before use.
1 M acetic acidDilute 57 ml of glacial acetic acid to 1000 ml with water.
Dilute potassium iodobismuthateDissolve 100 g of tartaric acid in 500 ml of water and add 50 ml of potassium iodobismuthate solution R1. Store protected from light.
DetectionUV light, 254 nm. For identification, then spray with dilute potassium iodobismuthate.

Trainer’s note: the GF254 layer glows under UV 254 nm, so spots that absorb UV look dark. The spray turns amines orange.

4 · Identification

Mobile phase. 1 M acetic acid : methanol : ethyl acetate (20 : 30 : 50).

Solution (1). Extract finely powdered tablets equivalent to 5 mg of chlorpheniramine maleate with chloroform. Filter, evaporate the filtrate to dryness with minimum heat, and dissolve the residue in 1 ml of chloroform.

Solution (2). Dissolve 50 mg of chlorpheniramine maleate working standard (WS) in chloroform and dilute to 10 ml.

Procedure.

  1. Apply 2 µl of each solution on the origin line.
  2. Develop until the solvent front has moved at least 15 cm from the origin.
  3. Remove the plate, mark the front, dry in air and examine under UV 254 nm.
  4. Spray with dilute potassium iodobismuthate.

Trainer’s note: one average tablet weight of powder holds 4 mg of drug. Use the average tablet weight to work out how much powder holds 5 mg.

5 · Related substances

Mobile phase. Diethylamine : chloroform : cyclohexane (10 : 40 : 50).

Solution (1). Extract finely powdered tablets equivalent to 50 mg of chlorpheniramine maleate with chloroform. Filter, evaporate the filtrate to dryness, and dissolve the residue in 1 ml of chloroform.

Solution (2). Dilute 1 volume of Solution (1) to 500 volumes with chloroform.

Procedure.

  1. Apply 10 µl of each solution on the origin line.
  2. Develop until the solvent front has moved at least 12 cm from the origin.
  3. Remove the plate, mark the front, dry in air and examine under UV 254 nm.
  4. Disregard any spot remaining on the line of application.

Trainer’s note: judge each secondary spot against the Solution (2) spot, not against the main spot. A mobile phase that is too weak or too strong leaves every spot bunched at one end.

6 · Calculation of Rf

Rf = distance moved by the spot ÷ distance moved by the solvent front

Measure both distances from the origin line. Report Rf to 2 decimal places.

Hints: four ideas
Silica is polar. Polar compounds stick to it and move less.
Rf = distance moved by the spot ÷ distance moved by the solvent.
Under UV 254 nm the plate glows green. Spots look dark.
Iodobismuthate spray turns amines orange.
