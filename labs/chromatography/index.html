Find 4 mg in a 90 mg tablet.

A chlorpheniramine maleate tablet holds only 4 mg of drug, mixed with filler, colour and traces of impurities.

Before a batch is released, a QC laboratory must prove the drug is there, show that impurities are low, and measure how much each tablet contains. It does all three by making the mixture race: chromatography.

The basic idea · watch

Stick, let go, move on.

Every chromatography method has a stationary phase that stays still and a mobile phase that moves over it.

Molecules keep jumping between the two, and they move forward only while they are in the mobile phase. A compound that sticks to the stationary phase more often falls behind. That is what separates compounds.

Colour code on every animation: red circle = polar compound (has an –OH), blue hexagon = non-polar compound, purple dots = solvent.

The basic idea

Like sticks to like.

How strongly a compound is held depends mostly on polarity. A polar stationary phase (such as silica, covered in –OH groups) holds polar compounds. A non-polar stationary phase (such as C18 chains) holds non-polar compounds. Tap each card.

Change the polarity of the mobile phase and you change how fast compounds move. You will use this in every technique.
The basic idea · watch all three

Three techniques, one principle.

TLC: liquid climbs a plate. Compounds end up in different places: you measure Rf.

HPLC: liquid is pumped through a packed column. Compounds come out at different times: you read retention times.

GC: a gas carries vapour through a hot column. Also separated in time, but only for compounds that evaporate without breaking down.

Tap TLC, HPLC or GC above the animation to switch. All three need a clean, dissolved sample first.

Extraction · CLO1

Get the drug out, leave the rest behind.

Extraction moves the drug into a solvent it dissolves in well, where the excipients dissolve poorly. Tap each excipient to see how it is handled.

Extraction · liquid–liquid

Two liquids that do not mix.

In liquid–liquid extraction (solvent extraction), the sample is shaken with two immiscible liquids, usually water and an organic solvent. Each compound partitions: it divides itself between the layers according to where it dissolves better.

Non-polar, un-ionised molecules go to the organic layer. Polar and charged molecules stay in the water. The layers are then run off separately.

Chloroform is denser than water, so it forms the lower layer. Ether and hexane float on top.

Extraction · try it

Change the pH, move the drug.

Chlorpheniramine is a base (pKa about 9.2). In acid it takes an H+, becomes charged and stays in water. In alkali it is the neutral free base and moves into the organic layer. Drag the slider.

Extraction · a classic scheme

Wash in acid, extract in alkali.

Watson shows how pH switching cleans up an organic base, such as cyclizine in an injection:

Dissolve in dilute acidthe base is charged: stays in water
Shake with etherneutral and acidic excipients leave
Make alkalinethe base becomes neutral
Extract into organic solventthe drug moves; salts stay
Back-extract into acidready for UV or HPLC

For an organic acid the rule flips: high pH keeps it in water, low pH sends it into the organic layer.

Watson (2017), Fig. 15.1

Extraction · solid-phase (SPE)

A tiny column that catches the drug.

In solid-phase extraction, the sample passes through a small cartridge packed with an adsorbent, often C18 silica. Four steps:

  1. 1Condition with methanol, then water or buffer.
  2. 2Load the sample in a weak solvent: the drug is held on the solid.
  3. 3Wash with a weak solvent: interfering compounds rinse through.
  4. 4Elute with a small volume of a strong solvent: the drug comes off clean.

Watson (2017), Fig. 15.6

Check

Three questions on extraction.

QUESTION 1 OF 3
You want chlorpheniramine (a base, pKa 9.2) in the chloroform layer. The water layer should be at about:
pH 11, about 2 units above the pKa, so almost all of the base is un-ionised and moves into chloroform. At pH 2 and pH 7 it is mostly charged and stays in water.
QUESTION 2 OF 3
In SPE, which step removes the interfering compounds?
The wash. A weak solvent rinses interferents through while the drug stays on the solid. Eluting comes after, with a strong solvent.
QUESTION 3 OF 3
Tablet powder is shaken with chloroform and filtered. What stays on the filter paper?
The polar filler does not dissolve in chloroform, so it stays on the filter. The drug passes through in solution.
TLC · instrumentation · CLO1

A plate, a tank and capillary action.

  1. 1Spot a few µl of each solution on a pencil origin line, about 1 µl at a time, drying between spots.
  2. 2Develop: stand the plate in a closed tank with the mobile phase below the origin. The solvent climbs by capillary action.
  3. 3Mark the front in pencil as soon as the plate comes out, then dry it.
  4. 4Detect the spots under UV light or with a spray reagent.

Watson (2017), Fig. 13.1

TLC · mobile phase · watch

The solvent competes for the silica.

On silica, the mobile phase and the compounds compete for the same polar –OH sites. A more polar solvent takes more of them, so compounds stick less and move further.

Solvents are ranked by polarity in the elutropic series: hexane (0) → toluene → dichloromethane → chloroform → ethyl acetate (4.4) → methanol (5.1) → water.

Too weak: everything stays near the origin. Too strong: everything runs to the front. Real methods mix solvents to land the drug at Rf about 0.3–0.7. Polarity index: Watson (2017), Table 13.2

TLC · the number on the plate

Rf: how far, compared with the solvent.

Rf = distance moved by the spot ÷ distance moved by the solvent front, both measured from the origin. It is always between 0 and 1. Measure to the centre of the spot.

Check

Three questions on TLC.

QUESTION 1 OF 3
The solvent moved 10 cm and a spot moved 4 cm. Its Rf is:
4 ÷ 10 = 0.40. A value above 1 means the division was upside down; 0.60 is the distance the spot did not travel.
QUESTION 2 OF 3
Every spot stayed on the origin. What should you change?
A more polar (stronger) mobile phase competes for the silica and moves the spots up.
QUESTION 3 OF 3
On a GF254 plate under UV 254 nm, a drug spot looks:
The drug absorbs the UV and blocks the plate’s green glow, so it shows as a dark spot. Orange spots come from iodobismuthate spray.

Reading a plate · CLO2

Identity: match the standard.

The sample and a reference standard run side by side on the same plate. The test passes when the principal spot of the sample has the same Rf and the same colour or reaction as the standard.

For chlorpheniramine maleate tablets, the plate is checked twice: under UV 254 nm, two spots (the drug and maleic acid) must match the standard; after iodobismuthate spray, the orange amine spot must match.

Comparing on one plate cancels out day-to-day changes in Rf.

Reading a plate · CLO2

Limit test: is the impurity spot too big?

A concentrated sample is run beside a dilute standard. Any secondary spot in the sample must be no more intense than the standard spot.

The dilution sets the limit. Watson’s example: 1% w/v hydrocortisone acetate beside 0.01% w/v hydrocortisone gives a limit of 0.01 ÷ 1 × 100 = 1%. Here the impurity spot is bigger, so the sample fails.

In the chlorpheniramine method, Solution (2) is Solution (1) diluted 1 in 500: a 0.2% limit. The spot left on the origin (maleic acid) is disregarded.

Watson (2017), Fig. 13.7

Reading a plate · practice

Pass or fail?

Each plate: lane 1 is the concentrated sample, lane 2 is the 0.2% standard. Ignore the spot on the origin.

PLATE 1 OF 3
The secondary spot is smaller and fainter than the standard spot, so the impurity is below 0.2%.
PLATE 2 OF 3
The secondary spot above the main spot is larger and darker than the standard spot: the impurity is above 0.2%.
PLATE 3 OF 3
The only extra spot is on the origin. The method says to disregard it, so the sample complies.
Reading a plate · applications

Where TLC earns its place.

TLC is used for identity checks on raw materials, impurity limit tests and cleaning checks in manufacturing. Open both sections.

Cheap and robust. Many samples run on one plate. Nearly any compound can be seen with the right spray. The whole chromatogram is visible, so nothing can hide by never leaving the column, as can happen in HPLC or GC.

Less separating power than HPLC. Limited sensitivity. Not for volatile compounds. Results depend on operator skill. Spot size is a rough guide to amount, so for measuring content, HPLC is used. (High-performance TLC with a densitometer can measure, but is less common.)

Watson (2017), Ch. 13 keypoints

Check

Two questions on reading plates.

QUESTION 1 OF 2
2 µl of a 1% w/v sample is run beside 2 µl of a 0.02% w/v impurity standard. What limit does this set?
0.02 ÷ 1 × 100 = 2%. 0.02% is the standard’s concentration, not the limit; 50% comes from dividing the wrong way round.
QUESTION 2 OF 2
A sample spot has the same Rf as the standard but does not turn orange with iodobismuthate. The standard does. Conclusion?
Identity needs the same Rf and the same reaction. Two different compounds can share an Rf by chance.
HPLC · instrumentation · CLO1

Pump, inject, separate, detect.

  1. 1Pump: pushes filtered, degassed mobile phase at a steady flow (e.g. 1 ml/min) under high pressure.
  2. 2Injector: a loop, often 20 µl, puts the sample into the flow as one band.
  3. 3Column: a steel tube packed with 3–10 µm particles, usually C18.
  4. 4Detector (usually UV) and data system: peaks, areas, report.

Chlorpheniramine assay: 150 × 4.6 mm C18 column, 5 µm packing, 1.0 ml/min, 20 µl, UV 254 nm. Watson (2017), Ch. 12

HPLC · stationary phase · watch

Flip the phases, flip the order.

Normal phase: polar silica, non-polar mobile phase. Polar compounds are held by adsorption and come out last.

Reverse phase: silica coated with C18 (octadecylsilane, ODS) chains, with a polar mobile phase such as methanol–water. Compounds partition into the oily layer: the more non-polar, the longer they stay.

ODS and similar phases are used for more than 80% of pharmaceutical analyses. Watson (2017), Fig. 12.2 and Table 12.2

In reverse phase, the organic solvent is the strong part of the mobile phase. A useful rule (the "rule of 3"): 10% less methanol makes retention about 3 times longer. Move the slider.

HPLC · ionisable drugs · watch

A charged drug slips through C18.

Most drugs are acids or bases, so mobile-phase pH changes how charged they are. A charged molecule is polar and is held poorly by C18.

The chlorpheniramine method runs at pH 3.6, where the drug carries a + charge. Its buffer contains sodium hexanesulfonate: the oily tail sits in the C18 layer and the (−) head pairs with the drug. This is an ion pair.

Silica-based columns are usually used between about pH 2 and 8.5. Above that the silica slowly dissolves. To keep a base like chlorpheniramine (pKa about 9.2) fully neutral you would need pH 11 or more, so methods use pH control together with ion pairing instead. (Watson, 2017, Ch. 12)

HPLC · the output

Time says what; area says how much.

  • t0Dead time: when an unretained compound (or solvent) reaches the detector.
  • tRRetention time: when the peak comes out. Compared with a standard, it identifies the compound.
  • k′Capacity factor = (tR − t0) ÷ t0: how many times longer the drug spends in the stationary phase than in the mobile phase.
  • APeak area is proportional to the amount injected. The sample’s area is compared with a standard’s area.

Watson (2017), Ch. 10

HPLC · before any sample

Prove the system works before you trust it.

Each day the standard is injected several times. The results must meet the method’s limits before any sample is injected. These are the limits in the chlorpheniramine method:

ParameterLimitChecks
Tailing factor≤ 2.0Peak shape. Tailing upsets the area.
Plate count (N)≥ 1500Column efficiency: narrow peaks.
RSD of peak area≤ 2.0%Injection precision.
RSD of retention time≤ 1.0%Steady flow and mobile phase.
k′≥ 2.0The peak is well away from t0.

N = 5.54 (tR ÷ W½)², where W½ is the peak width at half height. Narrower peaks give more plates. Resolution between two peaks, Rs = 2(tRB − tRA) ÷ (WA + WB), uses baseline widths; Rs above about 1.2–1.5 means full separation. (Watson, 2017, Ch. 10)

Check

Three questions on HPLC.

QUESTION 1 OF 3
On a C18 column, which of these elutes last?
The most lipophilic (non-polar) compound partitions most into the C18 layer, so it comes out last.
QUESTION 2 OF 3
t0 = 1.5 min and tR = 7.5 min. What is k′?
(7.5 − 1.5) ÷ 1.5 = 4.0. Dividing 7.5 by 1.5 without subtracting gives 5.0.
QUESTION 3 OF 3
The RSD of the standard’s peak area is 2.8% (limit 2.0%). What do you do?
No sample results count until the system passes. Find the cause (e.g. a bubble in the injector), fix it, and repeat.

GC · principle · CLO1

If it can fly, GC can separate it.

In gas chromatography the sample is vaporised and carried by a gas through a heated column. Compounds separate by how long they spend dissolved in the liquid stationary phase on the column wall.

Good for

Residual solvents, volatile impurities, volatile oils, fatty acids, compounds with no UV chromophore.

Strength

Capillary columns separate far better than HPLC. The gas needs no disposal.

Limit

Only volatile, heat-stable compounds. Others need derivatising first. No water or salts.

Watson (2017), Ch. 11 keypoints

GC · instrumentation · CLO1

Carrier gas, hot injector, oven, detector.

The sample evaporates in the hot injector. A split injection sends only a small part (e.g. 1 in 10 to 1 in 100) onto the capillary column, so it is not overloaded.

A carrier gas (helium or nitrogen) sweeps the vapour through the column. The oven can rise in a temperature programme (e.g. 10 °C/min), so compounds with very different boiling points all come out in reasonable time.

The usual detector is the FID, a small hydrogen flame. Watson (2017), Fig. 11.1

GC · stationary phase · CLO1

Packed or capillary: a coat of liquid either way.

PackedCapillary

Lower oven temperature improves resolution. Resolution grows with the square root of column length, so twice the resolution needs a column four times longer. A thicker film holds volatile compounds longer. (Watson, 2017, Ch. 11)

Watson (2017), Ch. 11

GC · choosing a phase

From oily silicone to polar wax.

Most GC stationary phases are silicone polymers. Swapping methyl groups for phenyl or cyanopropyl groups makes them more polar. Carbowax (polyethylene glycol) is the most polar common phase.

Like sticks to like again: a non-polar phase (OV-1) separates mainly by boiling point; a polar phase (Carbowax) holds polar compounds such as alcohols longer.

GC · in pharmacy

The chloroform must not stay in the tablet.

Solvents used to make a drug can remain in it. ICH Q3C sorts them by risk, and GC measures them:

ClassMeaningExample limit
Class 1AvoidBenzene 2 ppm
Class 2LimitChloroform 60 ppm · methanol 3000 ppm
Class 3Low toxicityEthanol 5000 ppm

Headspace GC is the usual method: the sample is sealed in a vial and heated, and only the vapour above it is injected. The drug and excipients never enter the column. (ICH Q3C; Watson, 2017, Ch. 11)

Check

Two questions on GC.

QUESTION 1 OF 2
Which sample is not suitable for direct GC?
GC needs volatile, heat-stable compounds. A salt that decomposes cannot be vaporised intact.
QUESTION 2 OF 2
The most widely used GC detector for organic compounds is:
FID responds to almost all C–H compounds over a wide range. UV is the usual HPLC detector; TCD is universal but less sensitive.

Check your understanding

Five questions, no marks recorded.

QUESTION 1 OF 5
A spot moved 4.5 cm; the solvent front moved 15.0 cm. The Rf is:
4.5 ÷ 15.0 = 0.30. 3.33 is the division upside down.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
To extract a basic drug from water into an organic solvent, the water should be:
In alkali the base is un-ionised, so it moves into the organic layer. In acid it is charged and stays in water.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
On a C18 column, methanol is raised from 55% to 65%. The drug peak:
Methanol is the strong solvent in reverse phase. 10% more makes retention about 3 times shorter.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
In the chlorpheniramine related-substances test, a spot stays on the origin line. You:
The method says to disregard the spot on the line of application. In the basic mobile phase, maleic acid does not move.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
Residual chloroform in a drug substance is best measured by:
Chloroform is volatile, so headspace GC measures it without injecting the drug. It has no useful UV chromophore for HPLC.
Take home

Prepare, separate, detect, decide.

1
Extraction moves the drug into a solvent the excipients avoid. pH decides which layer an acid or base goes to; SPE catches, washes and releases it.
2
TLC: silica plate, solvent by capillary action, UV or spray detection. HPLC: pumped liquid, C18 column, UV detector. GC: carrier gas, coated capillary in an oven, FID.
3
Rf = spot ÷ front. Identity needs the same Rf and the same reaction as the standard; a limit test compares a secondary spot with a dilute standard.
4
No HPLC result counts until system suitability passes.
