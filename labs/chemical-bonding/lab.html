Bench card: the method
Molecule Bench · Pharmacy

Based onChang, Chemistry (10th ed.), Chapters 9–11

1 · Count: the Lewis structure

  1. Total valence electrons = the central atom + every outer atom.
  2. Shared pairs: 1 per single bond, 2 per double bond.
  3. Lone pairs on the central atom: its valence electrons, minus one per bond it makes (two for a double bond), then halve.

2 · Shape: VSEPR and hybrids

Domains = atoms bonded to the central atom + its lone pairs. A double bond is one domain.

Every bond has one σ bond; each double bond adds one π bond.

3 · Bond: ΔEN

ΔEN = larger EN − smaller EN

Below 0.5: non-polar covalent. 0.5 up to 2.0: polar covalent. 2.0 or more: ionic.

4 · Polarity

  1. No polar bonds: non-polar.
  2. Polar bonds in a symmetric shape, identical outer atoms, no lone pairs on the centre: the dipoles cancel, non-polar.
  3. Otherwise: polar.

5 · Forces between molecules

  1. London dispersion: in every molecule; stronger with more electrons.
  2. Dipole–dipole: polar molecules.
  3. Hydrogen bonding: H bonded to N, O or F (donor) meets a lone pair on N, O or F (acceptor).

6 · Decision

  1. GC, non-polar column: lower boiling point, earlier peak.
  2. Solubility: like dissolves like. Molecules that hydrogen-bond with water mix with it.
  3. Boiling point: stronger forces, higher boiling point. Hydrogen bonding raises it most.
  4. Leaks: molar mass below 29 g/mol rises; above 29 sinks.

Hints: four ideas
Count valence electrons, not atomic numbers.
A double bond is one domain for the shape.
Shape decides whether bond dipoles cancel.
Hydrogen bonding needs H on N, O or F.
