One tablet, two questions.

A chlorpheniramine maleate (CPM) tablet holds 4 mg of drug. Before a batch is released, QC asks: is the drug really chlorpheniramine? And does it dissolve the way it should?

Both answers come from shining light through the sample. Infrared (IR) light gives a fingerprint that proves identity. Ultraviolet (UV) light measures how much drug is in a solution.

Light and molecules · slide it

Shorter wavelength, more energy.

Light is a wave. The distance between two peaks is its wavelength. The shorter the wavelength, the more energy each bit of light carries.

UV (200–400 nm) and visible light (400–700 nm) carry enough energy to move electrons. IR light (2.5–25 µm) carries less: it can only make bonds vibrate.

IR is usually given as a wavenumber in cm−1: the number of waves in 1 cm. Wavenumber (cm−1) = 10 000 ÷ wavelength (µm). A bigger wavenumber means more energy.

Light and molecules · watch both

Lift an electron, or shake a bond.

A molecule takes in (absorbs) light only when the light's energy matches a change the molecule can make.

UV light lifts an electron in a bond to a higher energy level. IR light makes a bond stretch or bend more strongly.

The light that is absorbed is missing when it comes out of the sample. A spectrum shows which wavelengths went missing, and how much.

UV/visible · principles

The part that absorbs.

A chromophore is the part of a molecule that absorbs UV or visible light. It is a system of conjugated double bonds: double bonds separated by single bonds.

The longer the conjugated system, the longer the wavelength it absorbs and the stronger it absorbs.

The most common chromophore in drugs is a benzene ring. Chlorpheniramine has two aromatic rings: a chlorobenzene and a pyridine.
UV/visible · principles · tap both

pH can move the peak.

An auxochrome is a group with a lone pair of electrons, such as –OH or –NH2, attached to a chromophore. It changes how the chromophore absorbs.

In alkali, the phenol –OH of phenylephrine loses its proton and gains an extra lone pair. The peak moves to a longer wavelength (a bathochromic shift) and gets stronger (a hyperchromic shift).

So a UV method must always state its solvent and pH. Tap both solvents to continue.

UV/visible · principles

Measure at the top of the peak.

A UV spectrum plots absorbance against wavelength. UV peaks are broad and smooth.

The wavelength of the highest point is λmax. For chlorpheniramine in dilute hydrochloric acid it is about 265 nm. (USP; manufacturer's STP)

Measuring at λmax gives the biggest reading. The top of the peak is also flat, so a small error in the wavelength setting changes the reading the least.

An electron can jump to many slightly different vibrational levels of the excited state. Each jump needs a slightly different energy, and they overlap into one broad band. (Watson, Ch. 4)

UV/visible · principles · slide it

Twice the drug, twice the absorbance.

Light of intensity Io goes into the cell, and It comes out. The absorbance is A = log (Io ÷ It).

The Beer–Lambert law: A = εbc. ε is a constant for the drug at that wavelength, b is the path length of the cell (usually 1 cm), and c is the concentration.

So absorbance rises in a straight line with concentration. That is what lets UV measure how much drug is present.

The slider uses carbimazole at 291 nm (Watson, Self-test 4.2).

UV/visible · instrument · watch

Lamp, split, sample, detect.

  1. 1Lamps. A deuterium lamp for UV (190–350 nm), a tungsten or quartz-halogen lamp for visible light (350–900 nm).
  2. 2Monochromator and slit. Splits the light into its wavelengths; the slit lets one narrow band through.
  3. 3Sample cell. Made of quartz, which lets UV light through.
  4. 4Detector. A photomultiplier or photodiode measures the light that comes out.

UV/visible · in QC · step by step

Three steps to the answer.

20 tablets weigh 1.656 g · powder taken 0.5195 g · 40 mg per tablet · extract 500 ml · dilution 5 → 250 ml · A = 0.596 · A(1%, 1 cm) = 580
STEP 1 OF 3
How much furosemide should the 0.5195 g of powder contain?
0.5195 ÷ 1.656 × 40 mg × 20 = 251.0 mg expected.
STEP 2 OF 3
What is the concentration of the diluted solution in the cell?
0.596 ÷ 580 = 0.001028 g/100 ml = 1.028 mg/100 ml.
STEP 3 OF 3
What is the content as a % of the stated amount?
1.028 × 50 = 51.40 mg/100 ml; × 5 for 500 ml = 257.0 mg found. 257.0 ÷ 251.0 × 100 = 102.4%.
UV/visible · in QC

Measure the standard beside the sample.

Many methods do not use an A(1%, 1 cm) value. Instead, a working standard of known concentration is read on the same instrument, at the same time, in the same solvent.

Your chlorpheniramine STP uses this for dissolution: a 0.008 mg/ml standard in 0.01 N HCl, read at 265 nm beside each sample. (manufacturer's STP)
UV/visible · in QC

Does the drug come out in time?

A dissolution test shows that the drug is released from the tablet. UV measures how much has dissolved.

STP settingChlorpheniramine 4 mg tablets
Apparatus2 (paddle), 50 rpm, 37.0 ± 0.5 °C
Medium500 ml of 0.01 N hydrochloric acid
Time30 minutes, then filter a sample
MeasureUV absorbance at 265 nm against the standard
Limit (Q)Not less than 80% of the label claim dissolved

Stage 1 (S1): 6 tablets. Each must reach at least Q + 5% = 85%. If not, test 6 more (S2): the average of all 12 must be at least Q, and no tablet below Q − 15%. (USP <711>)

IR · principles · watch all three

Light atoms, strong bonds, high wavenumber.

Think of a bond as a spring joining two balls. Each bond vibrates at its own rate and absorbs IR light of that energy.

Lighter atoms vibrate faster, so bonds to hydrogen (O–H, N–H, C–H) absorb at the highest wavenumbers. Stronger bonds also vibrate faster: a C=O double bond absorbs higher than a C–O single bond.

Order (Watson): O–H > N–H > C–H > C≡N > C≡C > C=O > C=C > C–O > C–C > C–F > C–Cl.

IR · principles · tap each card

No dipole, no band.

IR light is absorbed only when the vibration changes the bond's dipole (its uneven share of electrons).

Source: Watson, Ch. 5.

IR · principles · tap each region

Groups on the left, fingerprint on the right.

IR spectra run from 4000 to 400 cm−1, high energy on the left. Bands point down because the y-axis is % transmittance.

The carbonyl band tells you what kind.

C=O gives a strong band that is easy to spot. Its position shows what sort of carbonyl it is. (Watson, Fig. 5.9)

Which bond absorbs at the higher wavenumber?
O–H (about 3200–3550 cm⁻¹). Bonds to hydrogen absorb highest.
QUESTION 2 OF 3
A strong band at about 1750 cm⁻¹ most likely belongs to:
Unconjugated ester C=O absorbs at about 1750 cm⁻¹, as in aspirin's acetyl group.
QUESTION 3 OF 3
Why does nitrogen gas (N₂) not absorb IR light?
Both atoms are identical, so the vibration does not change a dipole.
IR · instrument · watch

All wavenumbers in one second.

A dispersive instrument uses a monochromator and reads one wavenumber at a time. A spectrum takes 2–3 minutes.

An FTIR instrument uses an interferometer instead. A moving mirror makes all wavenumbers interfere at once, giving an interferogram. A calculation, the Fourier transform, turns it into a spectrum in about 1 second.

Many scans can be averaged, so the spectrum is less noisy. (Watson, Ch. 5)

IR · instrument · try it

A plastic film checks the scale.

Before identity tests, the wavenumber scale is checked with a polystyrene film. Its bands sit at known positions, for example 1601.2 and 1028.3 cm−1, each allowed ±1.0 cm−1. (Ph. Eur. 2.2.24, via Shimadzu)

Your instrument reads 1601.9 and 1027.6 cm⁻¹. Is it fit to use?
The differences are 0.7 cm⁻¹ each, within ±1.0. The scale is fit to use.

QUESTION 1 OF 2
In an FTIR instrument, what replaces the monochromator?
An interferometer, with a moving mirror.
QUESTION 2 OF 2
Why is the IR sample held in salt (KBr, NaCl), not glass?
Glass absorbs strongly in the IR. Alkali-metal halides such as KBr and NaCl let IR light through.
IR · sample preparation

Four ways to hold a sample.

KBr or KCl disc

1–2 mg of drug ground with 300–400 mg of dry KBr, pressed into a clear disc. The usual pharmacopoeial method. Use KCl for hydrochlorides, to avoid halide exchange.

Nujol mull

Drug ground into liquid paraffin and spread between NaCl plates. Nujol adds bands near 3000 and 1400–1500 cm−1. Gentle, so it is used for polymorphs.

Liquid film or cell

A liquid sample, or a solution in chloroform, between NaCl or KCl plates. Never water: it dissolves the plates.

ATR

The sample is pressed on a crystal and IR light reflects inside it. Little preparation. Works for creams and gels.

Source: Watson, Ch. 5. A disc press uses about 800 kPa under vacuum.

IR · fingerprint identification

Same compound, same spectrum.

In pharmaceutical analysis, IR is a fingerprint test. Each compound has its own pattern, so a match with a reference confirms identity. (Watson, Ch. 5)

Chlorphenamine maleate: Ph. Eur. identifies it by IR against the chlorphenamine maleate CRS. For tablets, USP extracts the drug base into hexane first, then compares it with the reference treated the same way. (Ph. Eur. 0386; USP)
IR · fingerprint identification

One small change, a new fingerprint.

Dexamethasone and betamethasone differ only in how one methyl group points, at carbon 16. Their fingerprint regions are still clearly different. (Watson, Fig. 5.15)

The crystal form matters too. If a pure substance does not match its reference, both can be recrystallised from the same solvent and run again.

Take home

Light in, answers out.

1
UV light lifts electrons in chromophores; IR light makes bonds vibrate. Shorter wavelength, higher wavenumber, more energy.
2
A = εbc. In QC: c (g/100 ml) = A ÷ A(1%, 1 cm), or compare with a standard read beside the sample.
3
Lamp → monochromator → quartz cell → detector, zeroed on a blank and checked with dichromate, holmium, toluene and KCl.
4
FTIR uses an interferometer. Choose the right sample preparation, then match the fingerprint below 1500 cm⁻¹ against the reference.
