1 · Purpose and scope

To show that chlorpheniramine maleate is released from the tablets in 30 minutes.

2 · Specification (USP)

Not less than 80% (Q) of the labelled amount of chlorpheniramine maleate is dissolved in 30 minutes.

StageTestedAcceptance
S16Each unit not less than Q + 5% (85%).
S26 moreAverage of 12 not less than Q (80%); no unit less than Q − 15% (65%).
S312 moreAverage of 24 not less than Q; not more than 2 units below Q − 15%; no unit below Q − 25% (55%).

Trainer’s note: failing S1 is not a batch failure. It means you test more tablets.

3 · Dissolution conditions

Apparatus2 (paddle), 50 rpm
Medium0.01 N hydrochloric acid, 500 ml, at 37.0 ± 0.5 °C. Dilute 0.85 ml of hydrochloric acid to 1000 ml with water.
Time30 minutes
Wavelength265 nm (λmax), quartz cells, medium as blank

4 · Standard solution

  1. Weigh accurately about 20 mg of chlorpheniramine maleate working standard (WS) into a 100 ml volumetric flask.
  2. Dissolve in about 30 ml of medium and dilute to volume with medium.
  3. Dilute 4 ml of this solution to 100 ml with medium. Filter.

Final concentration: about 0.008 mg/ml.

Trainer’s note: there are two dilutions. Use the weight you actually weighed, not “20 mg”.

5 · Test solution and measurement

  1. Place one tablet in each of six vessels and start the apparatus.
  2. After 30 minutes, withdraw 25 ml from each vessel and filter through a membrane filter.
  3. Measure the absorbance of the standard and of each test solution at 265 nm, against medium.

Final concentration for full release: 4 mg in 500 ml = 0.008 mg/ml.

6 · Calculation

% dissolved = (Aspl ÷ Astd) × (Std wt ÷ 100) × (4 ÷ 100) × 500 × (P ÷ 100) × (100 ÷ 4)

Aspl, Astd: absorbance of the test and standard solutions. Std wt: weight of WS (mg). P: % potency of the WS (as is). 500: volume of medium (ml). The last 4: label claim (mg).

Trainer’s note: with 20.0 mg of a 100.0% WS, the formula is simply (Aspl ÷ Astd) × 100.

7 · Linearity check (training)

Dilute 2, 4, 6, 8 and 10 ml of the 100 ml WS solution to 100 ml with medium. Read each at 265 nm and fit a straight line A = m·c + b. The line is acceptable if r² is at least 0.999.

Concentration of a test solution: c = (A − b) ÷ m. % dissolved = c × 500 ÷ 4 × 100.

Trainer’s note: this section is a training exercise to show the Beer–Lambert law. Routine release uses the single standard in section 4.

Before you start: today’s procedure

The line at the bottom of the screen and the glowing box show your next move. STP, beside Hints, is the method you work from: it opens at the section you need now. Hints explains the ideas behind each step.

Hints: four ideas
Measure at λmax: the top of the peak, about 265 nm.
Beer–Lambert: absorbance is proportional to concentration.
Quartz lets UV through; glass blocks it below about 320 nm.
Compare each sample with the standard read beside it.
