Bench card: the method
Reaction Bench · Hospital pharmacy
Unitsmg ÷ (g/mol) = mmol · 1 mmol of HCl = 1 mEq
Relative atomic massesH 1, C 12, N 14, O 16, Na 23, Mg 24, Al 27, S 32, Cl 35.5, K 39, Ca 40, Fe 56, Cu 64, Zn 65, Ag 108, Ba 137
Based onChang, Chemistry (10th ed.), Chapters 3 and 4

1 · Dispensary: doses in mmol

n (mmol) = mass (mg) ÷ M (g/mol)
  1. Work out M of the salt from the formula.
  2. mmol in one tablet (or in 5 mL) = mg ÷ M. One KCl gives one K⁺; one NaHCO₃ gives one HCO₃⁻; one CaCO₃ gives one Ca²⁺.
  3. Tablets a day = prescribed mmol a day ÷ mmol per tablet. Give whole tablets.
  4. Liquid: mL a day = prescribed mmol ÷ mmol per mL.

2 · QC lab: reaction types

TypePattern
CombinationA + B → AB
DecompositionAB → A + B
Single displacementA + BC → AC + B
Double displacementAB + CD → AD + CB
Neutralisationacid + base → salt + water
Combustionfuel + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O

Redox: electrons move. Look for an element on either side, or O₂ reacting. Ions that only swap partners (double displacement, neutralisation) are not redox.

3 · Drug information: balance the equation

  1. Correct formulas first. Never change a subscript.
  2. Balance the metal, then Cl, then H, and O last.
  3. Use the smallest whole numbers, then recount every atom.
  4. The coefficients are mole ratios, not mass ratios.

4 · Drug information: acid-neutralising capacity

mmol HCl = mmol antacid × (HCl coefficient ÷ antacid coefficient)
  1. mmol antacid in one tablet = mg ÷ M.
  2. Use the mole ratio from your balanced equation.
  3. For HCl, 1 mmol = 1 mEq.
  4. Theory is the maximum. A measured value can be lower, never higher.

5 · Manufacturing unit: the limiting reagent

3NaHCO₃ + C₆H₈O₇ → Na₃C₆H₅O₇ + 3H₂O + 3CO₂
SubstanceM (g/mol)
Sodium bicarbonate, NaHCO₃84
Citric acid (anhydrous), C₆H₈O₇192
Carbon dioxide, CO₂44
  1. mmol of each reactant = mg ÷ M.
  2. Divide each by its coefficient (3 and 1). The smaller result is the limiting reagent.
  3. CO₂ (mmol) = 3 × that smaller result. Mass (g) = mmol × 44 ÷ 1000.
  4. Excess left = mmol present − mmol used. Change to mg with × M.
  5. The formula keeps citric acid in slight excess.

6 · QC release: percentage yield

% yield = actual ÷ theoretical × 100
  1. CO₂ released = balance reading before − reading after.
  2. Theoretical CO₂ comes from station 5.
  3. In-house limit (simulated for training): release only if CO₂ is at least 90% of theoretical.
  4. Over 100% means a slip. Recheck the division.

Hints: four ideas
Every station passes through moles. Divide the mass by the molar mass first.
Coefficients are mole ratios, not gram ratios.
The smaller mass is not always limiting. Compare moles ÷ coefficient.
Percentage yield is actual ÷ theoretical. Over 100% is a warning sign.
